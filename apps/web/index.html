What we are not

We are not a government body, and we do not track load shedding. We are ordinary people making a sheet that is already public easier to read.

How? You ask.

DESCO and DPDC publish

Dhaka Electricity Supply PLC posts one sheet per weekday. Dhaka Power Distribution Company posts one per NOCS zone, 36 of them. Both are ruled tables of feeders against an hourly grid.

We read all of them

Not one file. Every DESCO weekday sheet and every DPDC zone sheet, re-checked four times a day. Where a sheet is a scan rather than a document, we say so and link it instead of guessing.

You look

Drop a pin. See the feeders near you and the hours they are scheduled off.

What "publicly available" means Two things, and only two: the load-shedding sheets DESCO and DPDC publish on their own websites, and OpenStreetMap place data. No private feeds, no scraping of anyone's account, nothing invented. Where we are unsure, we say so out loud.

This is an estimate. Here's why.

No distributor publishes a "which feeder am I on" lookup, and none release feeder boundaries as map data. So we infer, and we would rather show the workings than pretend.

Why a shortlist, not "your feeder"
Your point is scored against distributor areas, zone centres, and each feeder's own written area description. Several feeders legitimately describe overlapping streets, so we show the closest matches instead of picking one and calling it yours.
Why there's never a percentage
A score can always be printed as a percentage; that doesn't make it a probability. We'd need a verified address-to-feeder dataset to earn one. Until that exists, confidence is capped at Medium and no number is shown.
A published window is not a guarantee
These are probable windows. Power can stay on right through one, or go out well outside one. Generation, faults, weather and maintenance all decide on the day. Nothing here is a promise of supply.
How to get your exact schedule
Your bill names your distributor and your S&D or NOCS office. That office's own sheet is the authoritative one.
  1. Call 16999 and choose your distributor, or call them direct, numbers are under Let them tell you.
  2. Open your distributor's own load-shedding page from Sources and find your office's sheet.
  3. Ask your building manager or nearest office which feeder your meter sits on. Once you know the name, this site becomes exact rather than estimated.

Let them tell you

We cannot notify anyone. Your distributor can. Register with them, on their own site, using your own meter details.

Never enter your bill or account number here We never ask for it and can't register you for anything.
